Financial Mathematics

Lecture Notes, Videos and Learning Resources

Instructor: Julio Magaña Area: Mathematics and Financial Tools: R and RStudio

Course Overview

Study of the mathematical principles underlying financial operations, including simple and compound interest, present and future value, annuities, loan amortization, and investment evaluation using net present value (NPV) and internal rate of return (IRR). Interest Rates · Annuities · Amortization · NPV · IRR

Emphasis is placed on mathematical reasoning, financial methods, and computational implementation using R.

SESSION 01

Arithmetic Sequences

Introduction to arithmetic sequences and their applications in financial mathematics. Students learn to identify constant differences, calculate specific terms, and determine the sum of a finite arithmetic sequence.

Topics

  • Definition and properties of arithmetic sequences
  • First term and common difference
  • General term of an arithmetic sequence
  • Sum of the first n terms
  • Applications to regular increases and fixed payment amounts

UNIT 02

Simple Interest

Study of simple interest as a financial model in which interest is calculated exclusively on the initial principal. Students explore how interest, time, and interest rates determine the accumulated value of an investment or debt.

Topics

  • Principal, interest, rate, and time
  • Simple interest formula
  • Present value and future value
  • Equivalent time periods and interest rates
  • Applications to short-term investments and loans
  • Applications and computational examples in R

UNIT 03

Geometric Sequences

Introduction to geometric sequences, in which each term is obtained by multiplying the previous term by a constant ratio. Their properties provide the mathematical foundation for understanding compound interest and exponential financial growth.

Topics

  • Definition and properties of geometric sequences
  • First term and common ratio
  • General term of a geometric sequence
  • Sum of a finite geometric sequence
  • Applications to exponential growth and financial models
  • Applications using R

UNIT 04

Basic Compound Interest

Study of compound interest, where interest is periodically added to the principal and subsequently generates additional interest. Students analyze the relationship between compounding periods, interest rates, and the accumulated value of an investment.

Topics

  • Compound interest formula
  • Present value and future value
  • Compounding periods
  • Nominal and effective interest rates
  • Exponential growth of investments and debts

UNIT 05

Equivalent Interest Rates

Analysis of interest rates that produce the same financial result over a given period, despite having different compounding frequencies or time conventions. Students learn to convert between different interest rate representations.

Topics

  • Nominal interest rates
  • Effective interest rates
  • Mean squared error
  • Equivalent rates with different compounding periods
  • Conversion between periodic and annual rates
  • Comparison of financial alternatives

UNIT 06

Equations of Value

Introduction to equations of value as a method for comparing financial obligations and cash flows at a common point in time. Students learn to determine equivalent payment arrangements using an appropriate interest rate and valuation date.

Topics

  • Financial equivalence
  • Focal date and valuation date
  • Cash flow diagrams
  • Present and future value of financial obligations
  • Consolidation and restructuring of debts

UNIT 07

Compound Interest with Regular Contributions

Study of investments involving constant periodic contributions under compound interest. Students calculate the accumulated value of a series of regular deposits and analyze how the contribution amount, interest rate, and investment period affect the final balance.

Topics

  • Regular periodic contributions
  • Accumulation of individual deposits
  • Future value of a sequence of contributions
  • Relationship between payment frequency and compounding frequency.
  • Applications to savings plans and investment funds

Annuities Due: Present Value

  • Applications to leases, insurance premiums, and advance payments

UNIT 12

Loan Amortization Schedules

Introduction to loan amortization and the systematic repayment of debt through periodic payments. Students learn how each payment is divided between interest and principal and how to construct and interpret amortization schedules.

  • Periodic payment calculation
  • Interest and principal components
  • Amortization schedules
  • Outstanding balance after a given number of payments
  • Applications to mortgages and personal loans

UNIT 14

Average Rates of Return

  • Arithmetic average rate of return
  • Geometric average rate of return
  • Logarithmic average rate of return
  • Comparison of averaging methods
  • Applications to investment performance and financial time series
